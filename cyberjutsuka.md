# Cyberjutsuka — The Way of the Practitioner

A **Cyberjutsuka (サイバー術家)** is a practitioner of Cyberjūtsu. The way of the practitioner brings technical learning, mental discipline, and moral growth together through practice with others.

> Cyberjūtsu aims to become an educational method of digital growth, mental growth, and moral growth at the same time.
>
> — [Principle of Cyberjūtsu](./principle.md)

This guide connects the practitioner's mindset with conduct in the dojo and daily digital life. Quotations reproduce the repository's wording; historical attributions are retained as recorded in [principle.md](./principle.md). Practical explanations apply those ideas to the course experience.

**In this guide:** [Mindset](#mindset) · [Moral code](#the-moral-code-in-practice) · [Behaviour](#behaviour-during-practice) · [Learning](#from-technique-to-principle) · [Reflection](#reflection-and-daily-life)

## Mindset

### The Cyberjutsuka's goal

Cyberjutsu is a continuing educational practice in which attack and defense help people improve. Approach each session with five intentions:

- **Develop yourself and contribute to society.** Bring what you learn into your relationships and digital life.
- **Make the best use of your energy.** Seek an appropriate, effective action and understand why it works.
- **Seek mutual prosperity.** Your progress and your partner's progress belong together.
- **Create the conditions for shared learning.** Think about what your partner needs to practise and improve.
- **Look beyond the technical result.** Develop attention, judgment, self-control, and habits that remain useful after the course.

> Do not forget that the objective of jūdō study is to improve every day,
> not to win or lose.
>
> — Jigorō Kanō, as quoted in [Principle of Cyberjūtsu](./principle.md)

### Two principles to carry into every exchange

| Principle | Meaning | What it asks of the practitioner |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Seiryoku zen yo](./glossary.md#seiryoku-zen-yo) | Maximum efficiency with minimum effort. | Observe, prepare, and choose an action suited to the situation. Practise until it becomes precise and repeatable. |
| [Jita kyōei (自他共栄)](./glossary.md#jita-koei) | Mutual welfare and benefit. | Help your partner learn, exchange roles, and make the practice useful to both of you. |

Efficiency includes the use of attention, timing, and effort. Mutual aid gives that efficiency an educational purpose. These principles guide both attack and defense, as explained in [Principle of Cyberjūtsu](./principle.md).

## The moral code in practice

> Spirit first, technique second.
>
> — Gichin Funakoshi, as quoted in [Principle of Cyberjūtsu](./principle.md)

The [Cyber Moral Code — Saibā-dō (サイバー道)](./moral-code.md) describes the practitioner's inner attitude and behaviour. Its ten values are listed below, with practical applications for a course.

| Value from the moral code | Application during practice |
| --- | --- |
| **Courage** | Take part, attempt the exercise, and acknowledge what you still need to learn. |
| **Sincerity** | Describe what you observed and understood honestly, including uncertainty and mistakes. |
| **Etiquette** | Greet the dojo, teacher, and partner; give instructions and demonstrations your attention. |
| **Honor** | Take responsibility for your actions and respect the agreed conditions of practice. |
| **Humility** | Make room for learning, including from a less experienced partner. |
| **Self-control** | Manage frustration, act deliberately, and stop when instructed. |
| **Mutual Aid** | Give your partner the opportunity and support needed to progress. |
| **Perseverance** | Repeat patiently, even when an exercise feels difficult or familiar. |
| **Integrity** | Follow the exercise's constraints and remain honest about your results. |
| **Kindness** | Treat yourself and your partner with care when either of you struggles. |

> Put your ego aside to get along with the others.
>
> — [Cyber Moral Code: Humility](./moral-code.md)

## Behaviour during practice

### Learn with the Sensei

A **Sensei (先生)** guides learning, transmits principles, and maintains discipline. Practising with a Sensei means making a serious effort to understand the way being taught: its techniques, sequence, and mindset.

**The four commitments of the course are:**

1. **Use only the techniques and commands taught by the Sensei during the course.** Practise within the exercise's constraints, even when you already know other methods.
2. **Participate in all activities requested by the Sensei.** The activities form a learning sequence; each contributes to understanding the teaching.
3. **Practise with mutual aid.** We are here to become better together. Use your experience to support the exchange and your partner's progress.
4. **Follow the Sensei's instructions carefully.** Listen through the demonstration, then ask questions to clarify what you need to understand.

The [training etiquette](./rules.md#training-etiquette) also asks practitioners to wait for instructions before using the cyberdeck, and to ask before entering or leaving the dojo during training.

### Give your partner a useful practice

Cyberjutsu develops shared improvement through adversarial cybersecurity exercises. Technical knowledge is useful when it serves that learning. Overwhelming a partner immediately with unfamiliar techniques can prevent the exercise from teaching what it was designed to teach.

**Shiai is not Randori.** Keep the distinction between competition and training clear. In **Randori (乱取り)**, engage sincerely, try the techniques, adapt, and learn from resistance. A successful exchange gives both partners something to understand and practise again.

The roles help structure that exchange:

- **Tori (取り)** applies the technique being studied.
- **Uke (受け)** receives it and provides the conditions needed to study it.

These roles change with the exercise. Tori may be practising defense while Uke creates an incoming connection. Exchange roles so that both practitioners learn both sides. See the [introductory course](./courses/esgi-2026/short-introduction-cyberjutsu.md#key-elements-of-practice).

For example, in [Yomi no Kata](./kata/yomi-no-kata.md), Uke generates a bounded activity and Tori observes and explains it. One of its success criteria is:

> Tori explains the event without guessing beyond the observed evidence.
>
> — [Yomi no Kata: Success criteria](./kata/yomi-no-kata.md#success-criteria)

Here, helping the partner means providing the agreed activity, allowing observation, and repeating it so understanding can improve.

### Respect the dojo and its signals

The **Dōjō (道場)** is a shared space for disciplined practice. In Cyberjutsu, its digital environment is a closed network disconnected from other networks. Only the cyberdecks of partners engaged in the exercise may be targeted.

The [fight rules](./rules.md) require equal starting hardware, software, and configuration; available practice services; and authorised credentials. During sparring, allow the exchange to happen: engage in attack and defense, and avoid sustained blocking that prevents the partner from practising.

| Signal | Required response |
| --- | --- |
| **Hajime** | Begin or resume on the instructor's signal. |
| **Mate** | Stop actions and return to the starting position, with a clear prompt and no running actions remaining. |
| **Sono mama** | Hold the current situation without performing new actions. |
| **Sore made** | End the exchange and stop actions. |

Practise **Rei (礼)** before and after the exchange: greet your partner and thank them. The repository gives **Onegaishimasu** before practice and **Domo arigato** afterward. Follow the Sensei's instructions for the formal group greetings. See [greetings and etiquette](./rules.md#etiquette).

## From technique to principle

> Enter by the technique, progress to the principle.
>
> — Jigorō Kanō, as quoted in [Principle of Cyberjūtsu](./principle.md)

A **Waza (技)** is a teachable, repeatable action. Cyberjutsu uses short command lines or command sequences that can be learned, memorised, explained aloud, and typed by hand. Their value includes the understanding that makes them appropriate to a situation.

> As an art, it should represent the right action at the right time, with the most accurate and effective result possible.
>
> — [Waza: technique](./techniques.md)

### Give repetition your attention

The repository's courses use repetition and role exchange to build familiarity and precision. A familiar command is an opportunity to refine observation, preparation, timing, and control. Follow the repetitions set by the Sensei, including when you can already perform the command.

| Form of practice | What to develop |
| --- | --- |
| **Uchikomi** | Refine entry, timing, and precision through repeated practice. |
| **Nagekomi** | Repeat the complete technique, including its effect. |
| **Kata (形)** | Study a principle through a structured, repeatable form. |
| **Yakusoku geiko (約束稽古)** | Practise with agreed roles, actions, and constraints. |
| **Randori (乱取り)** | Apply techniques with adaptation and resistance inside the rules. |

Definitions: [Glossary](./glossary.md). Practical examples: [Cyberjutsu kata](./kata/README.md) and the [introductory course](./courses/esgi-2026/short-introduction-cyberjutsu.md).

### Let understanding develop through practice

The repository describes **Shuhari (守破離)** as a progression:

- **Shu (守):** preserve and learn the fundamentals and forms.
- **Ha (破):** move beyond strict imitation to understand variation and principle.
- **Ri (離):** internalise the learning and act naturally without clinging to form.

Use this to understand why careful imitation comes before independent variation. During a course, follow the scope the Sensei has set for the exercise. Source: [Shuhari](./glossary.md#shuhari).

## Reflection and daily life

> It is necessary to consider others’ reactions to you in your self-reflection.
>
> — Jigorō Kanō, as quoted in [Principle of Cyberjūtsu](./principle.md)

**Mokusō (黙想)** is a moment of quiet reflection to integrate the experience and knowledge acquired during practice. At the end of training, follow the Sensei's instructions for reflection, greetings, and leaving the dojo. Sources: [Mokusō](./glossary.md#mokuso) and [greeting after training](./rules.md#greeting-after-the-training).

The following questions apply the repository's principles to personal reflection:

- What did I observe before acting, and what did I assume?
- Where did preparation or timing improve the result?
- How did my actions help or hinder my partner's learning?
- How did I respond to difficulty, correction, or being defeated?
- What will I practise more carefully next time?
- What habit can I carry into my daily digital life?

The way continues through **Tsune (常)**, the habit of sustained practice:

> Continuous practice as a permanent ritual to make progress.
>
> — [Glossary: Tsune](./glossary.md#tsune)

Carry the same attention into everyday use of computers and the internet: observe before reacting, acknowledge what you do not understand, choose appropriate actions, and help others learn. The final aim expressed in [Principle of Cyberjūtsu](./principle.md) is a better life in cyberspace.
