# NotebookLM infographic prompt

Use with [source.md](./source.md). Add the named original decks and poster as visual references when available. Copy the prompt below into the infographic generation instructions.

---

You are an expert in information design, educational communication, and branding. Create a single portrait infographic that welcomes participants at the beginning of any Cyberjutsu course. Use “Cyberjutsu course introduction — NotebookLM source” for all facts and wording. Write in English, with customary Japanese writing paired with rōmaji and a brief meaning. Make it readable as a handout or a large displayed image.

Build five ordered sections connected by a visual path:

1. **Meet WOCSA.** Expand the name, identify its nonprofit purpose, and show how country chapters and shared projects connect local action with international collaboration. Include a compact selection of project names and descriptions from the source. Use the WOCSA website and 2024/2026 decks' orange/white visual identity.
2. **Join us.** Invite everyone to help make the digital world safer. Give three concrete contribution examples and the action “Visit wocsa.org — find your chapter — join an activity.” Welcome novices as well as experts.
3. **Cyberjutsu: the digital martial art.** Explain martial arts applied to cybersecurity. Show the five benefits as short labels: coordination, acting under pressure, reflexes, adaptation, and respect. Include 精力善用 (Seiryoku zen'yō, best use of energy) and 自他共栄 (Jita kyōei, mutual benefit). Add a small evidence strip naming DEF CON 32/33 and Cité des sciences, Paris, with their years from the source.
4. **Practise together.** Include a “Cyberjutsuka Goal” panel focused on continued progress, contribution, efficient effort, helping the partner, and digital life beyond technique. Add a distinct panel headed “Hands-on Cyberjutsu rules to ensure success of the human experience.” Preserve all four rules: use only taught techniques and commands; participate in all activities; practise with mutual aid; follow the teacher carefully. Include a short closed-dojo reminder and a signal strip explaining Hajime (begin), Mate (stop and reset), Sono mama (hold), and Sore made (end).
5. **始め — Hajime!!!** Finish with “Be curious. Respect your partner. Learn together.” Make the start invitation the strongest closing element. Put wocsa.org, cyberjutsu.education, and the source's contact address in a readable footer.

Follow “Cyberjutsu Affiche 2026 (1).pdf” for sections 3–5: warm ivory, a subtle Japanese wave pattern, red accents, black headings, white rounded panels, dark footer, and the red/white/black braid turning into computer cables. Let this motif guide the reading order while leaving generous space around text. The goal and rule wording comes from the 2024 DEF CON deck via the Markdown source; the poster supplies their visual treatment.

Use short statements and clear visual hierarchy. Allocate the most space to participant goals and the four rules; shorten decorative copy before reducing their legibility. Use supplied logos faithfully, or plain-text brand names if original assets are unavailable. Keep captions and contact details readable. Use a visible URL unless a supplied QR code has a verified relevant destination.

Keep this reusable across courses. Omit the poster's old price, age range, timetable, registration offer, venue fields, and event-specific partners. Do not fabricate statistics, affiliations, event photographs, or testimonials. Keep historical examples clearly labelled and source attribution compact.
