# NotebookLM presentation prompt

Use with [source.md](./source.md). Add the named original decks and poster as visual references when available. Copy the prompt below into the presentation generation instructions.

---

You are an expert in communication, presentation support design, and branding. Prepare a presentation to introduce any Cyberjutsu course. Use “Cyberjutsu course introduction — NotebookLM source” as the factual and editorial source. Write everything in English; pair customary Japanese writing with rōmaji and a short English meaning.

Follow this narrative in order:

1. Present WOCSA, the Worldwide Open Cyber Security Association nonprofit: what it is, what it does, its projects, how it works, and its organisation. Use the visual style and wording of wocsa.org, 2024-WOCSA-CYBERJUTSU-EN, and 2026-WOCSA-CYBERJUTSU-EN. Explain the relationship between country chapters, local actions, and international projects.
2. Motivate the audience to join WOCSA and contribute to making the digital world safer for all of us. Keep the WOCSA visual identity. Give concrete ways to help and a clear next step through the chapter directory at wocsa.org. Make beginners feel welcome.
3. Introduce Cyberjutsu: what it is, why martial arts apply to cybersecurity, and examples from DEF CON and the Cité des sciences et de l'industrie in Paris. Follow the visual style and translated messaging of “Cyberjutsu Affiche 2026 (1).pdf.” Explain efficient practice and mutual benefit, and briefly show how a session works.
4. Prepare participants to enjoy the session. Include the headings “Cyberjutsuka Goal” and “Hands-on Cyberjutsu rules to ensure success of the human experience.” Use the goals and all four rules transcribed and copy-edited in the source. Their wording comes from the 2024 DEF CON deck; apply the poster's visual style. Explain respect, the closed dojo environment, role exchange, and the instructor's start/stop signals.
5. Motivate the audience to begin. Finish with a bold “始め — Hajime!!!” and “Let's begin!” in the poster's visual style. Hand over to the instructor.

Use the source's 12-slide sequence as a default for a 6–8 minute introduction. Give each slide one clear message, short readable text, and a purposeful visual. Put fuller explanations and source attribution in presenter notes or unobtrusive captions where supported. Keep the four workshop rules legible and complete.

Move from WOCSA orange, white, grey, and its globe/shield identity to the poster's ivory wave pattern, black headings, red accents, rounded white panels, dark footer, and braided belt becoming computer cables. Use authentic supplied logos without alteration. If original artwork is unavailable, follow the source's visual description and use plain-text brand names. Use Japanese glyphs accurately and readably.

Keep the presentation reusable: historical examples should remain labelled by event and year. Omit event-specific prices, schedules, age restrictions, sponsor panels, dated officeholders, and claims of prior approval. Do not invent statistics, testimonials, affiliations, or documentary event photographs. Keep technical command tutorials in the course itself. End on Hajime, with contact details placed before the final beat or in a discreet footer.
