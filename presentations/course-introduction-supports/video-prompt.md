# NotebookLM video prompt

Use with [source.md](./source.md). Add the named original decks and poster as visual references when available. Copy the prompt below into the video generation instructions.

---

You are an expert in educational communication, video storytelling, and branding. Create a welcoming 4–5 minute video to open any Cyberjutsu course, grounded in “Cyberjutsu course introduction — NotebookLM source.” Use English narration and on-screen text. Pair customary Japanese writing with rōmaji and a concise English meaning; pronounce the Japanese terms naturally without reading character explanations aloud.

Build a single coherent journey:

1. Introduce WOCSA: its nonprofit identity, mission, way of working, organisation, and project examples. Explain international collaboration and local chapters with a simple visual. Use wocsa.org and the 2024/2026 WOCSA decks' language and orange/white branding.
2. Invite viewers to join and contribute to a safer digital world. Suggest a few useful contributions, welcome beginners, and show wocsa.org as the route to a chapter. Transition from sharing knowledge to practising together.
3. Introduce Cyberjutsu as the digital martial art. Explain why martial arts training supports cybersecurity learning, efficient action, and mutual benefit. Show the roles of partner and instructor and the progression from repetition to freer practice. Include brief, labelled DEF CON and Cité des sciences, Paris examples from the source.
4. Explain “Cyberjutsuka Goal” and the “Hands-on Cyberjutsu rules to ensure success of the human experience.” Convey the goals and explicitly include all four rules: only taught techniques and commands; participation in all activities; mutual aid; careful attention to the teacher's instructions. Explain the closed dojo environment and the meanings of Hajime, Mate, Sono mama, and Sore made.
5. End with a warm, energetic handoff: “Be curious. Respect your partner. Learn together.” Show “始め — Hajime!” and say “Hajime! Let's begin!” The instructor gives the actual start signal.

Aim for roughly one minute on WOCSA and joining, two minutes on Cyberjutsu and examples, and one to two minutes on goals, rules, and the start. Adjust pacing for comprehension rather than rushing a rule card.

For Cyberjutsu scenes, use the translated messaging and visual language of “Cyberjutsu Affiche 2026 (1).pdf”: warm ivory, a subtle wave pattern, red/black/white, rounded panels, and a belt braided into computer cables. Where the format supports motion, let the belt-to-cable motif carry the transition. Otherwise use a clear sequence of illustrated scenes. Use concise on-screen text, calm transitions, and enough reading time. Narration should explain the scene rather than recite a dense slide.

Use only documented examples and authentic supplied branding. If artwork is unavailable, use the source's visual description and plain-text brand names. Label illustrations as illustrations when they could be mistaken for event footage. Keep prices, event timetables, sponsor claims, dated officeholders, and course-specific admission conditions out of this reusable opening. Do not add invented facts or testimonials. Present contact details before the final Hajime frame.
